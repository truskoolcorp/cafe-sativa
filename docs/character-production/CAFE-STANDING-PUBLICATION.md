Generation membership comes from a narrow character-owner definer bridge, not table grants. Existing generation candidate set remains finite1–32 with operational expiry; this is not infinite self-renewing permission.

`publication_authorities` links recorded plans to their standing scope, inserted in the same transaction. `authorized()` gains one conjunct for linked plans; absent links retain exact legacy approval semantics. Standing scope/admin/generation membership is rechecked at every preflight/claim, so revocation, expiry or binding retarget cannot leave unused recorded standing authority live. No consumed post is canceled; provider requests linearized before revocation can complete and require receipt reconciliation.

## Locks and revocation
Record and existing claim lock draft then approval. Publication guard then takes publication binding SHARE NOWAIT, scope UPDATE NOWAIT, generation binding SHARE NOWAIT, generation policy UPDATE NOWAIT, enrollment UPDATE NOWAIT. The existing scheduler_current reenters already-held policy/enrollment locks. NOWAIT on every cross-boundary lock prevents waiting draft→policy against existing planner policy→draft. Contention fails closed: recording raises and rolls back; the existing dispatch authorization wrapper catches guard errors and returns false, so claim returns NULL without an attempt. Neither path produces authority or schedules a retry. The caller must distinguish confirmed abort from uncertain transport completion and reconcile rather than blindly retry.

Publication binding disable/delete/retarget locks binding then disables old scope; no draft/approval locks. Generation retarget locks its binding then existing enrollment. A claim holding SHARE binding locks linearizes first and revocation waits; a revocation holding the binding makes a claim fail NOWAIT. Committed revocation is checked on the next fresh guarded read. Standalone admin removal is current checked, not physical cancellation of a consumed provider action.
